Conservative and Nonconservative Forces
(1 of 3)
A
conservative force
stores any work done against it and can
“give back” the stored energy by doing work itself:
The rock climber shown below does work against gravity as she climbs but can recover that work in the form of kinetic energy as she descends.
Nonconservative
forces convert (macroscopic) mechanical energy into (microscopic) internal energy:
The mover does work against friction that turns into heat and cannot be recovered as kinetic energy by returning the chest to its original position.

## Slide 4

Conservative and Nonconservative Forces
(2 of 3)
For a conservative force, the work done in moving between two points is independent of the path taken:
Because the work done by a conservative force is path independent, the work done in going around any
closed path
is zero:
For a nonconservative force, the path taken between two points must be specified in order to determine the work done by the force.

Potential Energy
The
“stored work” associated with a conservative force is called
potential energy

Potential energy is stored energy that can be released as kinetic energy.
The change in potential energy is defined as the negative of the work done by a conservative force acting over any path between two points:
